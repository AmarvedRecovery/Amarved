Research & Physiology

The Science of Recovery

We are guided by the latest research in neurobiology, psychoneuroimmunology, and autonomic physiology. Our approach is not just behavioral; it directly impacts cellular environments.

The Autonomic Nervous System

Chronic illness often involves a state of dysautonomia—an imbalance in the autonomic nervous system. The sympathetic branch (fight, flight, freeze) becomes hyperactive, while the parasympathetic branch (rest, digest, repair) is suppressed.

Our somatic tracking and breathing protocols are scientifically designed to increase vagal tone, signaling safety to the brain and allowing the body's natural cellular repair mechanisms to come back online.

Neuroplasticity & The Limbic System

The limbic system acts as the brain's alarm center. Prolonged illness or trauma can cause this alarm to get stuck in the 'on' position, interpreting mild exertion or harmless stimuli as severe threats, generating immense fatigue or pain as a protective mechanism.

Through targeted cognitive repatterning and micro-exposures, we utilize the brain's inherent neuroplasticity—its ability to form new neural connections—to manually reset this alarm system.
